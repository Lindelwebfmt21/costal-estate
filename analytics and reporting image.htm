What is a Business Intelligence Dashboard?

A business intelligence dashboard can be defined as an analysis tool that displays data from numerous sources on one screen. BI dashboards give businesses the tools to track essential performance indicators and streamline operations to meet their objectives. There are a lot of other important business intelligence dashboard features too that help organizations.  

They are a tech-driven methodology used to analyze and helpfully present information. Charts and graphs are combined in the business intelligence dashboard design to give viewers a comprehensive view of the scenario they are studying. By displaying the metrics pertinent to its role, industry, or platform, BI dashboard tools adapt to the demands of the individuals designing them rather than having a fixed, predetermined nature.  

Who needs a BI reporting dashboard anyways? A dashboard is useful for almost all businesses. Multiple could be useful for larger organizations to create attainable aims and goals for each department. 

One can help project managers monitor work, spot issues, and maintain projects on schedule and within budget by keeping track of activities, deadlines, costs, and other metrics. Project managers can monitor their projects' status with a project dashboard, which offers real-time metrics. 

1. Examples of Business Intelligence Dashboard

This image shows a brilliant Business intelligence Dashboard example. The resources and expenses have been shown by a circular color-coded graph in the bottom right corner. The activity is represented in a Bar graph, line graph, and pi graph. All these help the employees, executives, and the head to grasp the information at a glance and identify weak points in the business. Financial KPI dashboards, digital marketing dashboards, recruitment dashboards, web analytics dashboards, sales activity dashboards, executive marketing dashboards, pricing and supply dashboards, performance dashboards for email marketing campaigns, social media command center dashboards, and customer retention dashboards are a few common professional power BI dashboard examples. The options are essentially limitless! 

Business managers, corporations, and sales and marketing departments frequently employ sales and marketing open-source BI dashboards. These dashboards can have a wide range of capabilities. Still, their major attributes include the following: 

  • A customized user interface. 
  • A certain amount of interactivity. 
  • The ability to pull data in real time from many sources. 

They simplify data processing for our brains and give readers a quick overview of past, present, and future performance by helping the user to visualize otherwise complex and weighty raw data. 

2. Business Intelligence Dashboard vs. Business Reports

Information and dashboards are essential for business intelligence, and dashboards can make complex reports easier to grasp for users. For stakeholders that require glances at performance, dashboards are perfect. Reports are for stakeholders who want to slice and dice data to find insights and need additional information.

Business Intelligence Dashboard: Components and Features

BI dashboards are a technology-driven method of data analysis and visualization in an approachable manner. Best power BI dashboard designs include graphs and charts that give readers a complete picture of a firm's performance. BI dashboards provide business users with a consolidated view of pertinent and connected KPIs and trends for strategic planning and informed decision-making. Content creators can obtain quick overviews by combining charts, graphs, and reports on a single screen utilizing interactive components like filters and actions.

There are numerous capabilities available in business intelligence sales dashboard tools that may be utilized to automate tasks, enhance usability, and add visual appeal. Let's examine some of the main elements and functionality of contemporary BI Dashboards.

1. Interactive and customizable interface

The customizable interface enables users to create dashboards tailored to their needs. BI Reports allow viewers to access the data that underlies charts and visualizations for additional analysis, making them more interactive than static Excel reports. 

2. Common dashboard templates

Standard power bi dashboard templates already speed up the design process and enable users to import historical and current data. 

3. Connections to Data Sources

To combine data from many sources for analysis, BI Dashboards can link to Data Warehouses, Data Marts, Data Lakes, Operating Systems, etc. 

4. Data Visualizations

5. Drill-down Capabilities

These capabilities enable users to look closely at the underlying data by clicking on Data Visualization. This grants consumers access to more visualizations or data tables, typically organized hierarchically to allow users to drill down to different levels.

How to Create a Business Intelligence Dashboard?

Step 1: Integrate your data

You must first gather raw data and clean it up so it is analytics-ready before you can build any dashboards. Data from diverse sources must be mixed. Data replication, ingestion, and transformation must be carried out to merge different data types into standardized formats stored in a data lake or data warehouse. 

Step 2: Define your goals

The business aim you're attempting to assess, measure, and optimize must be defined explicitly. What step of your marketing funnel needs the most attention, or are you looking to identify new geographic areas to enter? 

Step 3: Keep visuals clean and simple

You may use your data to tell an engaging tale by maintaining a clear and straightforward visualization style. Select the graphs, charts, and maps that best describe your narrative by first taking the time to discover important KPIs and relationships. 

Step 4: Allow exploration and collaboration

Self-service Anyone in your organization may explore the dashboards and generate their insights thanks to BI. Instead of restricting users to linear searches, the most excellent solutions provide an associative engine allowing users to explore any links within the data freely. 

Step 5: Leverage AI

Augmented analytics, which combines AI and machine learning to support human intuition by providing insights and analyses and enabling search and natural language interaction, is included in modern BI dashboards.

Business Intelligence Dashboard Best Practices

1. Make communication, not good looks, your number one priority

You want to make it possible to make decisions more quickly and accurately using data and facts. 

It doesn't matter how attractive your dashboards are. Making an open source BI dashboard physically appealing makes it more appealing to the eye and might even boost the chance of initial acceptance. Still, it does not imply that it is carrying out its intended function. Your dashboard must convey the necessary information most quickly and transparently feasible. But being attractive is not the goal. 

2. Modern dashboards must be Web-based

You risk breaking data governance rules if you deliver business intelligence dashboards through a channel other than the web. It is also limiting. How else will you guarantee that the data you're receiving is consolidated, controlled, and trustworthy if your dashboards aren't Web-based? 

You can access real-time information using Web-based BI, enabling you to seize an opportunity or head off a problem. You can also share findings, start immediate conversations with other decision-makers, and spur quick action via web-based dashboards. You receive primary data and real-time data-related collaboration. 

3. Put the most important information above the fold

The most significant reports and information should be placed above the fold—the area of your screen that can be seen without scrolling—just as competent journalists place the essential news in the first lines of their articles. This enables quicker data analysis and comprehension. 

Make sure the most crucial reports are at the top if you can't fit everything above the fold, and don't make your users scroll very far to get to the end. Instead of a lengthy description of every data from every source that might be related to the topic, think of your dashboard as a quick glimpse to answer a question.  

4. Start with the big picture and drill into detail

Display data in a succession of a high-level summary report giving an immediate picture of what's going on in an organizational setting. 

Your dashboard needs to provide an overview that addresses a particular requirement or question, as was described in the first point. Users can drill down into more excellent information utilizing drill down, drill through, and drill anywhere to understand why something occurred, what is causing change, and where improvements can be made. 

5. Ensure natural alignment and logical order of charts

Information structure is the foundation for quick and thorough comprehension, and reliable data is the foundation of good knowledge. 

Charts are arranged in the dashboard to promote the quickest, most straightforward, and most logical information consumption. It is poor design, for instance, to put a sequence of five reports on a dashboard such that the user cannot wholly comprehend the first report's significance until they have read the fourth. This slows down information understanding.
